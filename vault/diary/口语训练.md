# 口语训练

### **🎯 6.5分到底什么水平？**

口语6.5分，允许你犯错，但要求你整体表达流畅、清晰。考官按四个维度打分，想拿6.5分，比较常见的组合是**两个维度到7分，两个维度到6分**。具体来看：

-   **流利性与连贯性 (6-7分)**：有持续表达的意愿，偶尔会因思考而重复或自我纠正，但整体连贯。能用一系列连接词，虽然可能不总是恰当。
    
-   **词汇多样性 (6-7分)**：有足够的词汇量来详尽讨论话题，虽然有时会用词不当，但能基本成功地进行**改述（Paraphrase）**，也就是换种说法表达同样的意思。
    
-   **语法多样性及准确性 (6分左右)**：能结合使用简单和复杂的句型，但在使用复杂结构时可能会出错，不过这些错误**极少造成理解困难**。
    
-   **发音 (6分左右)**：能清晰地表达，虽然可能有口音或个别单词发音错误，但**整体易于理解**。
    

总结一下：**大胆说，别怕错，重点是让考官听懂你**。

### **🧠 给你的"思维模板"：三步扩展法**

#### **1\. Part 1 & Part 3：观点-理由-例子/细节**

这是最经典、最实用的结构，**适用于所有需要你表达观点或偏好的问题**。

-   **第一步：直接回答 (Statement)**。明确说出你的观点，比如"Yes, I do." 或 "I prefer A."。
    
-   **第二步：给出理由 (Reason)**。解释你为什么这么想，用"Because..."开头。
    
-   **第三步：举例或补充细节 (Example/Detail)**。用一个具体的例子来支撑你的理由，或者补充一些相关的对比。
    

**举个栗子：**  
考官问："Do you like cooking?"  
你的回答可以这样组织：

-   **(观点)**: "Yes, I'm quite fond of it."
    
-   **(理由)**: "I find it really relaxing and creative, especially after a stressful day."
    
-   **(例子/细节)**: "For instance, last weekend I tried making Japanese ramen from scratch. It took a few hours, but the process was so therapeutic."
    

#### **2\. Part 2：5W1H 自我提问法**

Part 2需要你独自说2分钟，这是最容易卡壳的地方。拿到话题卡后，用**5W1H（Who, What, When, Where, Why, How）** 快速在纸上写下关键词，每个点说1-2句，内容就充实了。

**举个栗子：**  
话题是"描述一次你参加过的聚会"。

-   **Who**: 谁参加了？（我的高中同学们）
    
-   **What**: 什么聚会？（毕业五周年的同学会）
    
-   **When**: 什么时候？（上个月）
    
-   **Where**: 在哪里？（我们高中母校附近的一个餐厅）
    
-   **Why**: 为什么举办？（为了重聚，回忆旧时光）
    
-   **How**: 怎么庆祝的？（我们吃了饭，分享了各自这五年的经历，还看了以前的照片）
    

## 0814

> 跟读 15 分钟。Part1 只打 Hometown、Home、Accommodation，大约 12 题，全部录音。Part2 先不要上新类型，用「描述你的住处或家乡一处地方」练流程：笔记、说、听、改、重说。Part3 围绕 home 问 3 题，例如年轻人是否该离家、城市住房问题。

### **Part 1 跟读答案（12题，6.5分标准）**

**Hometown**

**1\. Where is your hometown?**  
My hometown is Jinan, which is the capital city of Shandong Province. It's located in the eastern part of China, not far from the coast.

**2\. Do you like your hometown?**  
Yes, I really do. I think it's a great place to live because it has a good balance of modern development and traditional culture. The people are also very friendly.

**3\. Have you always lived there?**  
Not really. I was born and raised there, but I moved away for university. Now I come back to visit my family during holidays.

**4\. Are there any changes in your hometown?**  
Definitely. It has changed a lot over the past ten years. There are many new shopping malls and apartment buildings, and the public transport system has improved a lot.

* * *

**Home**

**5\. Do you live in a house or an apartment?**  
I currently live in a flat, which is on the fifth floor of a modern building. It's not very big but it's comfortable for me.

**6\. Which room do you spend the most time in?**  
Probably my bedroom, because that's where I study and relax. I also have a small desk by the window where I do most of my work.

**7\. What can you see from the window of your home?**  
From my window, I can see a park with lots of trees and a small lake. It's quite peaceful, especially in the morning.

**8\. Do you want to move to another home in the future?**  
Yes, hopefully. I'd like to move to a bigger place with a garden someday, maybe when I have a family of my own.

* * *

**Accommodation**

**9\. Is it expensive to live in your accommodation?**  
It's not too bad compared to other cities, but rent has been going up recently. I share with a roommate, so that helps to reduce the cost.

**10\. What facilities are near your home?**  
There's a supermarket, a couple of restaurants, and a gym within walking distance. There's also a bus stop just outside the building.

**11\. Do you prefer living in a city or the countryside? Why?**  
I prefer the city because it offers more job opportunities and entertainment options. But I do enjoy visiting the countryside for a weekend getaway.

**12\. Is your home quiet or noisy?**  
It's generally quiet during the day, but sometimes it gets a bit noisy in the evening because there's a street market nearby. I've got used to it though.

* * *

### **Part 2 范文（6.5分标准）**

**Describe your home or a place in your hometown that you like.**

> I'd like to talk about my home, which is a flat in the city centre of Jinan. It's on the sixth floor of an old building, but it has been renovated recently, so everything looks fresh and clean.
> 
> In terms of appearance, it's not very large — there are two bedrooms, a living room, and a small kitchen. The living room has big windows that let in plenty of natural light, which makes the space feel bright and airy. I've decorated it with some green plants and a couple of pictures on the wall, so it feels quite cosy.
> 
> What I usually do there is pretty simple. Most of the time, I read books or watch movies on the sofa. Sometimes I invite a few friends over and we cook dinner together in the kitchen. It's not a fancy place, but it's where I feel most relaxed.
> 
> The reason I like it so much is that it gives me a sense of privacy and independence. After a long day at work or school, I can just close the door and unwind. Also, it's very conveniently located — I can walk to the supermarket and the metro station within five minutes. So overall, even though it's small, it's my safe space and I truly enjoy spending time there.

### **Part 3 范文（6.5分标准）**

**1\. Should young people leave their hometown to find better opportunities?**

> I think it depends on the person, but generally speaking, yes, it can be a good idea. In big cities, there are usually more job opportunities and better salaries, especially for young graduates. For example, someone working in IT or finance would probably have more career options in a city like Beijing or Shanghai. However, it's not for everyone — some people prefer to stay close to their family, and that's perfectly fine too. So I'd say it's a personal choice, but it's worth trying if you have a clear goal.

**2\. What are the main housing problems in big cities?**

> I think the biggest issue is the high cost of housing. Property prices and rents in big cities have become unaffordable for many young people. Another problem is the lack of space — apartments are often small and crowded, especially in older buildings. Also, traffic and noise can be a problem in residential areas near busy roads. So overall, while cities offer many advantages, housing is definitely a major challenge.

**3\. Do you think the government should do more to provide affordable housing?**

> Absolutely. I believe the government has a responsibility to ensure that ordinary people can afford a decent place to live. They could build more public rental housing or offer subsidies to first-time home buyers. Some cities have already introduced policies to control rising prices, but I think more needs to be done. It's a complex issue, though, because it involves balancing the interests of developers, local governments, and residents. But definitely, more action is needed.
